Pieces of Home is a free, cozy 3D browser game for players aged 10 and up, alone or with up to three friends.

The problem: people left many machines on the Moon and Mars. Some still work, like the Apollo 11 mirrors that scientists still bounce lasers off. Others went quiet. To most kids they look like junk, and their true stories are easy to miss.

Our solution: Remi's late dad built tiny models of real landing sites and wrote letters to their machines. Players step into each model and deliver letters to Apollo 11, Apollo 15, Surveyor 3, Opportunity, Ingenuity and finally Voyager 1. They play with the idea behind each machine, like corner mirrors, mesh wheels, or solar power and dust. Each idea rebuilds a part of Dad's robot, Milo. True story cards show real NASA photos with exact credits. Every fact in the game links to its source, and the build fails on an unsourced fact. Every model says "Model, not to scale". Co-op is kid-safe: private room codes, no chat, only emotes and preset phrases. No install, no account. A printable Field Guide helps classrooms.

NASA data: photos from the NASA Image and Video Library, NASA Photojournal and the Lunar Reconnaissance Orbiter Camera; the CGI Moon Kit (NASA Scientific Visualization Studio); the Viking Mars mosaic (NASA Ames, USGS); landing coordinates from NASA NSSDCA; Voyager 1 data from JPL Horizons; and NASA mission recordings.

Built with AI tools: Claude Code and an AI voice; see AI_USE.md.
