## Slide 1

Hi! We are Hackstreet Boys. Our project is Pieces of Home, a free browser game that tells the true stories of the machines people left on the Moon and Mars. Our challenge is Abandoned but not Forgotten: Storytelling about NASA's Discarded Equipment on the Moon and Mars.

## Slide 2

People have left many machines on the Moon and Mars. Some still do their jobs: scientists still bounce lasers off the mirrors that Apollo 11 left in 1969. Others went quiet, like the Opportunity rover, last heard from in June 2018. To most kids these machines look like old junk, far away, so their true stories are easy to miss.

## Slide 3

Our answer is a game with a heart. Remi's late dad built tiny models of real landing sites and wrote letters to the machines there. Each world is one cozy loop: pick a dome and a little rocket flies you there, deliver Dad's letter to a real machine, read its true story with a real NASA photo and source link, then play with the idea that made the machine work, like corner mirrors that send light straight back, and bring a new part home to Milo.

## Slide 4

The worlds are real landing sites: Tranquility Base with the Apollo 11 mirrors, Hadley-Apennine with the Apollo 15 Moon buggy, Meridiani Planum with Opportunity, Valinor Hills with Ingenuity, and a bonus trip to the Ocean of Storms. The finale points Dad's backyard dish at Voyager 1. NASA data is built in: photos from the NASA Image and Video Library, Photojournal and LRO, globe maps from the CGI Moon Kit and the Viking mosaic, real landing coordinates from NSSDCA, Voyager 1's light time from JPL Horizons, and real recordings like Mars wind from Perseverance. Every model says model, not to scale.

## Slide 5

We built accuracy into the code. Every number, date and claim in the game comes from one facts file, and every fact has its source link; the build fails if the game uses a fact without one. Real photos keep their exact NASA credit and Voyager 1's light time follows the date. It is also safe and easy: free in the browser with no install, account, ads or tracking, made for ages 10 and up. Friends join with a private room code and there is no chat, only emotes and kind preset phrases. There are captions, a calmer motion setting, a dyslexia-friendly font and a printable Field Guide for classrooms.

## Slide 6

Pieces of Home helps players see these machines as stories, not junk. It brings real NASA images, data and sounds into a game people want to finish, and it is ready for classrooms today. Next, we want to play-test it with players and teachers and add more machines. Left behind, but still answering, still teaching. And Voyager 1 is still sending.
